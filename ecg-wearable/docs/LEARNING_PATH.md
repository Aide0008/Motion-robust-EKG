# Learning path: from this project to a graduate-level understanding

The goal is that you can **derive, implement from scratch, and defend** every algorithm in this repo. Work through the stages in
order; each has something to build and something to check. Use the MATLAB scripts in `matlab/` and the Python in `analysis/` side by side.

## Stage 1 - Signals and sampling (1-2 weeks)
- Read: *The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing* (S. W. Smith, free at dspguide.com), chapters on the Fourier transform, sampling and filters. For depth: Oppenheim & Schafer, *Discrete-Time Signal Processing*.
- Build: in MATLAB, generate a 60 Hz sine plus 300 Hz sine, sample at 250 Hz, plot the spectrum and show the 300 Hz tone at 50 Hz (MATH.md section 1).
- Check: answer the section 1 questions in MATH.md; explain why a filter after sampling cannot undo aliasing.

## Stage 2 - Digital filters (2 weeks)
- Read: filter chapters in Smith; the RBJ "Audio EQ Cookbook" (the biquad formulas used here); Oppenheim & Schafer on IIR design and `filtfilt`.
- Build: reproduce `matlab/filter_chain.m`. Derive the notch's zeros by hand. Plot magnitude and phase of forward vs forward-backward filtering.
- Check: MATH.md sections 2-3. Predict, then verify, how a 0.5 Hz causal high-pass distorts a synthetic ST segment.

## Stage 3 - Statistics for noisy signals (1 week)
- Read: any robust statistics intro (median, MAD, why the median is robust).
- Build: show on simulated data that the std is wrecked by 1 % spikes while MAD/0.6745 is not (this is `test_mad_sigma_ignores_a_few_big_spikes`).
- Check: derive the 0.6745 constant from the normal CDF.

## Stage 4 - State estimation (3-4 weeks, the hard part)
- Read: Dan Simon, *Optimal State Estimation*, chapters on the Kalman filter and smoothing; Simo Särkkä, *Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing* (free from the author's site) - chapters on the Kalman filter, RTS smoother, and the continuous-discrete model (that is where $Q_d$ comes from). Original smoother: Rauch, Tung & Striebel, AIAA Journal 3(8), 1965.
- Build: implement the Kalman filter for a **constant-velocity** model, then for the integrated random walk used here; compare with `matlab/kalman_rts.m` and `analysis/ecgdn.py`. Then derive the discretised noise matrix.
- Check: MATH.md section 5, including the smoothing-spline result and the bandwidth table. Reproduce the table by feeding sine waves through the smoother and measuring gain.

## Stage 5 - ECG and biomedical instrumentation (2-3 weeks)
- Read: Webster (ed.), *Medical Instrumentation: Application and Design* (biopotential amplifiers, electrodes, interference); the AD8232 datasheet (Analog Devices); Kligfield et al., "Recommendations for the standardization and interpretation of the electrocardiogram: Part I", *Circulation* 2007 (filter bandwidth recommendations); Clifford, Azuaje & McSharry (eds), *Advanced Methods and Tools for ECG Data Analysis* (Artech House, 2006).
- Build: a table of the P, QRS, T wave durations and amplitudes, and the frequency content of each.
- Check: explain, from the amplifier's perspective, why electrode impedance mismatch raises mains hum (MATH.md section 8).

## Stage 6 - QRS detection and arrhythmia evaluation (2-3 weeks)
- Read: Pan & Tompkins, "A Real-Time QRS Detection Algorithm", *IEEE Trans. Biomed. Eng.* 32(3), 1985; the AAMI EC57 standard on testing arrhythmia detectors; Moody & Mark, "The impact of the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database", *IEEE EMB Magazine* 20(3), 2001.
- Build: install the `wfdb` Python package, download MIT-BIH records from PhysioNet, run `analysis/ecgdn.detect_beats`, and compute sensitivity and positive predictive value against the annotations.
- Check: MATH.md section 6; explain what a false positive and a false negative mean clinically.

## Stage 7 - Turning the prototype into evidence (ongoing; this is what a graduate admissions reader values)
1. **Public-data benchmark:** denoise records from the MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test Database at several SNRs; report SNR improvement and R-wave amplitude retention against a band-pass baseline and a wavelet baseline. Include the cases where yours loses.
2. **Ablations:** remove the QRS mask, then the noise meter, then the smoother (filter only); show what each contributes.
3. **Failure analysis:** keep the standing-test result in the repo (it is in RESULTS.md). Explaining a failure clearly is worth more than hiding it.
4. **Reproducibility:** every figure regenerated by a script; tests pass on a clean checkout; versions pinned.
5. **Safety and regulation literacy:** this is not a medical device. Be able to say what FDA 510(k) would require and why you are not claiming clinical performance.

## Interview-ready questions
- Why a Kalman smoother instead of a fixed low-pass or a wavelet threshold? When would the fixed filter win?
- What does the QRS mask buy you? Show the R-wave height with and without it.
- What did the standing test teach you about redundant electrodes?
- What would you do with 3 more months and a reference device?

*Verify citation details (volume, pages, years) before using them in an application.*
